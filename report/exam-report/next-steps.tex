\section{Next steps}

\subsection{Hyperparameter Tuning}
Exhaustive tuning on low-level architectural parameters is avoided to favor a limited set of high-leverage hyperparameters.
This is because the goal of the research is not to produce a \textit{State-of-the-Art (SotA)} model but rather to verify the robustness of the proposed design choices.
We ask ourselves if \textit{KD} provides gains to the training pipeline and if multimodal alignments help to create richer representations of the data.

    \centering

    \begin{tabular}{c|c|c}
        & Default              & Term                                  \\ \hline
        $\alpha$ & 1.0                  & Weight of the fusion loss term        \\

    \label{tab:hp}

The hyperparameters to tune are reported in table \ref{tab:hp}.
The $\alpha$ and $\beta$ terms are used to balance the loss which is aggregated from the fusion and \textit{KD} as:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \alpha \mathcal{L}_{fusion}  + \beta \mathcal{L}_{kd}$$

To reduce the search space we could fix $\alpha$ and only tune $\beta$.

\subsection{Ablation}
Once the hyperparameters tuning process is concluded, ablation is performed to assess the research question.
The elements we focus on for the process are \textit{KD} and modality fusion.

\paragraph{Knowledge Distillation} We assume the technique helps with the learning process by integrating information
encoded in the structure of the contrastive model of \textit{VATE}.
To see if the process really is of benefit we ablate it entirely.
This allows us to quantify its contribution.

We also ablate each modality independently, since text in the initial training experiments showed poor performance on our evaluation metrics.
If the teacher model has a weak performance on a modality, distillation from that branch may introduce noise rather than useful supervisory signal.

\paragraph{Modality fusion} Not all modalities showed strong performance in the initial training experiments.
It is possible that some modalities are too noisy or missing too often to extract information from.
For this reason we remove one modality at a time to evaluate its individual contribution to the fused representation.

To assess whether fusion enriches EEG representations we compare the full model with a version trained using EEG representations only.

To further see if the contrastive alignment is strong we ablate the MoCo process as well.
This ablation verifies whether improvements are due to improved negative sampling rather than multimodal integration alone.
We expect a drop in performance considering its contribution during training.

\subsubsection{Downstream tasks}
To really measure the effectiveness of the modality fusion we have to apply the trained model to a downstream task.

For a first approach we will produce a simple predictor on \textit{Valence-Arousal} using \textit{EEGAVI} as frozen backbone.
Given that we use all the data we can in the training of EEGAVI we have to exploit its validation and test sets.
By having \textit{EEGAVI} as frozen backbone, this does not lead to data leakage.

We also integrate data from \textit{DREAMER}, a dataset composed of EEG and ECG recordings that was not used in the training of \textit{EEGAVI}.

We expect \textit{EEGAVI} to outperform \textit{CBraMod} on the task for two reasons:
\begin{itemize}
    \item EEGAVI was pretrained on data drawn from the same sources
    \item The integration of multiple modalities favors richer embeddings than those computed by \textit{CBraMod}
\end{itemize}

Once this experiment is performed, a more complex one will be done on "A Multimodal Virtual Reality Data Acquisition Platform and Dataset to Assess Systemic Human Cognitive States"\cite{Aygun2025}.
The dataset is designed to measure human cognitive states in virtual reality environments.
The dataset focuses mostly on cognitive workload, mental fatigue and stress, metrics it tracks from self-assessments of the subjects.
We train two downstream predictors using \textit{EEGAVI} and \textit{CBraMod} as frozen backbone and compare their performances.
Our analysis aims to see if \textit{EEGAVI} was able to encode richer information on the affective cognitive state of a subject by the integration of multimodal information.

For future work a completely \textit{Flamingo}-like approach could be studied.
The current implementation is simpler as the fusion is only done at the end of the model's architecture.
Interleaving layers of CBraMod with cross-attention blocks could result in a stronger model as we feed the information gradually during the generation of the embeddings.
