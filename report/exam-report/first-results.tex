We applied early stopping on the global averaged value of \textit{mrr} with a patience of 12 epochs.
The training was stable as reported in the loss graph running for a total of n epochs.

The tracked metrics evaluate the results by measuring the capability to identify the originating modality from the fused vector.
This idea is a robust method for understanding if the final embedding correctly condenses the information from the various modalities.

An often used metric to measure this behavior is \textit{mean reciprocal rank (MRR)}.
It is a statistic that measures ranking systems by averaging the inverse rank of the first relevant result across multiple queries.
The value is defined in the range $MRR \in [0,1]$ with 1 indicating consistently being \textit{top1}:
$$MRR = \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{i=1}^{|Q|} \frac{1}{rank_i}$$

It is commonly used in information retrieval and scenarios where only the first correct answer matters.

Text exhibits significantly lower margin and MRR values, suggesting weaker cross-modal alignment.
Margin measures the relative separation between positives and negatives pairs in the embedding space.

Beyond the \textit{MRR} and \textit{margin} we also calculate the \textit{top-k} behaviors of the data.
All measurements depend on the size of the sample.
During training the value is moderated by the \textit{MoCo} queue and the batch size while in validation and test
it is the size of the dataset partition.
The test samples of our partitioned dataset amount to a total of: xxxx, thus making top-1 retrieval not trivial.
All results of the measured metrics are reported in table \ref{tab:metrics}.
For reference all modalities are compared with an EEG-only version to see if fusion helps in the process of retrieval.

A hyperparameter tuning process may improve reported performance.
